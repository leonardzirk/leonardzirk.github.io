Acclimation in Plants. The Green Hub is a research consortium built on one idea: the chloroplast is the cell's central coordinator for acclimation — how a plant adjusts to shifting light and temperature within its own lifetime.

It's both the sensor and the target. This page is your ticket in — no need to sit through all 250 papers.

🌿 Feature :: Why It's a "Hub"

Acclimation is reversible retuning — not evolution over generations, but a plant adjusting over minutes to days. The chloroplast plays three roles at once:

  • Sensor — photosynthesis is so light/temperature-sensitive that stress often registers here first (redox, ions, metabolites).
  • Target — to keep working under cold, heat or excess light, it rebuilds its own machinery — proteins, membranes, pigments, transporters.
  • Coordinator — it signals the nucleus (retrograde signalling) to change which genes the whole cell switches on.

🌿 Quick Poll

Which area fits you best?

Area A
Genetic Modulators
Chloroplast gene expression — which of its own genes are read & translated under stress.
  • How is translation tuned to stress?
  • How are RNAs processed & edited?
  • How are ribosomes built?
In the papers: translation & ribosome profiling, PPR/RNA-binding proteins, RNA editing, ribosome biogenesis.
Area B
Metabolic Modulators
Chloroplast metabolism & transport — what it makes and what crosses its membranes.
  • How do ions & sugars cross the envelope?
  • How is chlorophyll synthesis controlled?
  • How does redox keep it in tune?
In the papers: ion homeostasis, sugar/nucleotide transporters, tetrapyrrole biosynthesis, thioredoxins.
Area C
Signal Transduction
How the chloroplast's status is communicated — to the nucleus and across the cell.
  • How does the plastid signal the nucleus?
  • How do redox, ROS & ions carry info?
  • How is high-light defence triggered?
In the papers: retrograde/GUN signalling, redox & ROS, Ca²⁺ & jasmonate, anthocyanins.
Area D
Data Mining & Modelling
The computational backbone — turning many datasets into shared, reusable models.
  • How do we share data (FAIR)?
  • Can we model acclimation?
  • What makes analysis reproducible?
In the papers: PLANTdataHUB, kinetic & metabolic models, proteomics tools.
Service Z
The Shared Core
Central proteomics & metabolomics (Z1) for every group — the shared instrument behind Areas A–D.
Provides: absolute protein quantification, subcellular fractionation, systems-level measurement.

Glossary :: Words You'll Hear On Day One

Acclimation
Adjusting to change (cold, heat, light) within a lifetime — reversible, not genetic evolution.
Chloroplast
The green organelle where photosynthesis happens; the "hub" of it all.
Retrograde signalling
Messages sent from the chloroplast to the nucleus to change which genes switch on.
Tetrapyrrole biosynthesis
The pathway that builds chlorophyll — tightly controlled, easily damaged.
Thylakoid
The internal membranes inside the chloroplast where the light reactions run.
Proteostasis
Keeping proteins made, folded and disposed of — the job of chaperones and proteases.
Ionome
The full set of ions (K⁺, Ca²⁺, Fe…) in a compartment; their balance shapes acclimation.
FAIR data
Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable — the standard behind Area D.
Arabidopsis / Chlamydomonas
The main models: a small flowering plant and a single-celled green alga.
GUN pathway
"Genomes uncoupled" — the best-studied retrograde route from plastid to nucleus.
